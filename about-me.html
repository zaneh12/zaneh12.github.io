About

I'm Zane Hassoun.

These days my interests are in risk and forecasting, with a lot of attention on fixed income and rate-driven behavior. The problems I like most are the ones that cut across credit, rates, FX, and general quant-dev work: building systems that price, hedge, and explain what changes when markets move. Before that I spent a long time on a PhD in statistics, and the thread through all of it is the same: building models, code, and financial systems that still hold up when the inputs are messy and the assumptions need to survive contact with reality.

Current Job

Quantitative Risk in Digital Assets at Charles Schwab.

Most of my time at work sits around understanding the risks and failure modes in digital assets models. Working with different types of models from classical risk models to more modern machine learning approaches. Trying to understand how to structure and maintain risk models in a way that is robust to changing market conditions and new types of data.

What I like building

  • Risk engines and forecasting systems
  • Tools that make noisy data do something useful
  • Financial products and market-facing models
  • Software that stays readable after the first pass

Education

  • Ph.D. in Statistics, University of York
  • M.Sc. in Statistics, University of St. Andrews
  • B.A. in Economics, University of Massachusetts Amherst

What I work on

Forecast aggregation, prediction markets, market microstructure, and sequential decision problems where the environment changes over time. I also spend a lot of time thinking about fixed income hedging, MBS, and duration risk in rate environments that move quickly.

Teaching and work

I have taught probability, statistics, linear models, and practical data analysis modules across multiple academic years. The common thread is turning ideas into something that actually works.
